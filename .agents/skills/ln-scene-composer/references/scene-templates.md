# Scene templates

These are scene-clause starting points, not claims of proven image quality.
These examples illustrate quiet text-only defaults. Reference linework/palette,
established framing and the current background brief take priority; remove any
incompatible default clause rather than append contradictory instructions.
Use the named combinations in [camera-catalog.md](camera-catalog.md), then
complete the visible Shot Plan and single-frame test before assembling a prompt.
Replace character placeholders with verbatim Character Locks. Expand panel
templates using [panel-layouts.md](panel-layouts.md), including each panel's
active Background Contract. Do not leave placeholders in a delivered production prompt.

## 1. Fight

Default: **A. Layered Depth Staging + Waist-up + Low angle + 3/4 view**,
landscape 3:2 or portrait 2:3, Background Level 0-1.
Alternative: **D. Insert + Main**, Close-up + Eye-level + Side (profile) for an
essential hand/weapon detail, with the A main below.

```text
A character-focused action scene with a very simple background, layered depth
staging. Waist Up, Low Angle, 3/4 View, one shared level horizon. Character A
occupies the near left plane, turning to guard against Character B on a deeper
right plane. Their torso directions differ; overlap only non-acting silhouettes,
with depth-consistent apparent scale. Keep the short acting arm and guard
side-on and fully readable from each shoulder through elbow and wrist.
Use restrained diagonal speed lines and a small impact effect. Keep required
hands or an explicitly requested single weapon complete inside the crop.
Not a side-by-side lineup, not a flat profile view. The place is only plain
tone or one faint complete pillar, separated from heads and frame edges.
```

Assign left/right arms, targets and any weapon before using the clause. Do not
add swords, magic or props absent from the brief. A wide fight can follow the brief/reference or required full stance: explain the anatomy/background risk **before** its
prompt, then use Full body or Long shot as requested, with feet and headroom
protected. Dutch tilt is optional only for a justified action beat.

## 2. Everyday

Default: **A. Layered Depth Staging + Waist-up + Eye-level + 3/4 view**,
landscape 3:2, Background Level 1.
Alternative: **A + Bust-up + Eye-level + 3/4 view**, reducing visible joints
when the beat is a glance rather than a gesture.

```text
A calm everyday scene with a very simple background, layered depth staging.
Waist Up, Eye Level, 3/4 View, level horizon. Character A pauses in the near
left third and glances toward Character B on a deeper right plane.
A's near shoulder overlaps a small non-essential part of B's torso.
B is slightly smaller in apparent scale from depth and turns the torso toward A,
answering with a different expression. No held objects unless requested.
The setting is suggested only by one faint complete doorway outline, with
large empty tone areas and clear separation from heads and frame edges.
```

For school-age characters use natural complete outfits and youthful proportions;
do not add body emphasis or anatomical detail absent from the user's brief.

## 3. Dialogue

Default: **B. Dirty Single / Over-the-Shoulder + Bust-up + Eye-level +
Over-the-shoulder (dirty)**, portrait 2:3 or landscape 3:2, Background Level 0.
Alternative: **B + Bust-up + Eye-level + Over-the-shoulder (clean)**,
removing the foreground fragment when it blocks required evidence.

```text
A quiet dialogue with a very simple background. Dirty Single, Bust Up,
Eye Level, Over the Shoulder from behind Character B's right shoulder.
Only B's identifiable right shoulder enters the near right foreground,
slightly soft, with a coherent clothing edge; no random blurred mass.
Character A's sharp face is on the left third, looking toward B, with a
restrained expression appropriate to the line. Use one level horizon.
Keep A's face and any required gesture unblocked; no held objects by default.
The background is only a soft tone gradient with a gentle glow.
```

One focal face is intentional. If both simultaneous expressions are essential,
run the single-frame test and offer **C. Diagonal Split**, Bust-up + Eye-level
per panel, instead of forcing both frontal faces into this OTS.

## 4. Narration and atmosphere

Default: **F. Single Portrait + Long shot + Eye-level + Back view** for one
person; use **A. Layered Depth Staging** if several people are required.
Landscape 3:2, Background Level 2.
Alternative: the same technique + **Extreme wide + High angle + Back view**,
emphasizing smallness with simplified anatomy.

```text
A restrained atmospheric illustration with generous empty space.
Long Shot, Eye Level, Back View. Character A is a small complete figure
anchored on the lower right third, consistent back hair and clothing,
with clear margin around head and feet. Most of the frame is empty soft
sky tone. The setting appears as a faint, out-of-focus distant skyline
strip and one quiet complete landmark shape. Keep both much lighter than
the character, with a soft halo separating the silhouette.
```

Do not invent a skirt, ribbon or long hair that conflicts with the Lock.
A Long shot does not authorize detailed scenery or certification of tiny hands.

## 5. Comic physical contact

Default: **A. Layered Depth Staging + Bust-up + Eye-level + 3/4 view**,
landscape 3:2, Background Level 0.
Alternative: **D. Insert + Main**, Close-up contact insert + A Bust-up main.

```text
A comic back-slap, layered depth staging, Bust Up, Eye Level, 3/4 View.
The startled recipient is nearer; the laughing actor is one step farther,
slightly smaller and partly hidden by the recipient's near shoulder.
Turn the actor toward the receiving back. Keep the actor's named near-side
shoulder, short bent elbow, wrist and open palm readable against the
camera-side upper back. Protect both important expressions and contact;
use only a few motion ticks, an impact burst and plain tone.
Not a side-by-side lineup, not a flat profile view.
```

The anatomical left/right side must be filled from the plan, not guessed from
screen position. If the required faces/contact/grip cannot coexist naturally,
use D. The two golden variants are in [panel-layouts.md](panel-layouts.md).

## 6. Separate locations or two opposed viewpoints

Default: **C. Diagonal Split + Bust-up + Eye-level + 3/4 view per panel**.
Alternative: **C + Close-up + Eye-level + Front per panel**, simplifying
both reactions when place detail is unimportant. Background Level 0-1 per panel.

```text
One illustration divided into two views by one thin straight diagonal,
with the endpoints, region sizes and reading order specified in the plan.
Upper panel: Character A reacts in location A, Bust Up, Eye Level, 3/4 View.
Lower panel: Character B reacts in location B, Bust Up, Eye Level, 3/4 View.
The cameras belong to separate spaces; do not merge the locations or imply
direct eye contact across the border. No text, no speech bubbles,
panel borders are straight and do not overlap.
```

For two views of the same place, replace the location clauses with one named
event/instant and preserve world positions and costume/prop continuity.

## 7. Object reveal or magic

Default: **D. Insert + Main**: top **Close-up (detail) + High angle** of the
hand/object, bottom **A + Bust-up + Eye-level + 3/4 view** for two people
(or F for one). Background Level 0 per panel.
Alternative: **D** with a **Waist-up + Eye-level + 3/4 view** main,
giving a required grip more room.

```text
One illustration with two stacked panels, a top detail about one third of the
usable height and a bottom main about two thirds, separated by one black bar.
Top: Close Up detail, High Angle, the named owner's hand revealing one specified
object; complete essential fingers and object silhouette, no faces.
Bottom: Layered Depth Staging, Bust Up, Eye Level, 3/4 View, showing the
owner's expression and the deeper partner's distinct reaction to that same
object. Keep the anatomical hand side, prop design and clothing consistent.
No text, no speech bubbles, panel borders are straight and do not overlap.
```

Name the actual object from the brief. Do not add an artifact, glow or extra
equipment merely to make the reveal look dramatic.

## 8. Additional scene defaults

Every row supplies a starting technique/camera; preserve fixed source framing.

| Scene | Starting combination | Background / adjustment |
|---|---|---|
| Shock, embarrassment or chibi reaction | F + Close-up or Bust-up + Eye-level + Front; C if two separate reactions matter | Level 0; restrained effect marks |
| Walking together | A + Waist-up + Eye-level + Side (profile), or Back view | Level 1; one quiet prop; no default wide lineup |
| Eating / cafe | A + Waist-up + Eye-level + 3/4 view; D if the food/hand detail is the beat | Level 1; one table/cup arrangement within budget, at most one held object each |
| Night / moonlight | F for one, A for several + Bust-up + Eye-level + 3/4 view | Level 1-2 by brief; soft halo, not heavier rendering |
| Confession / emotional peak | F for one face, A for two + Close-up + Eye-level + 3/4 view; B OTS alternative | Level 0; important faces remain crisp |
| Formal serious single | E. Symmetry Framing + Waist-up + Eye-level + Front | Level 1; at most two light framing props with no tangents |
| Relationship distance | A + Bust-up foreground + Eye-level + 3/4 view | Smaller softer distant partner; justify stronger scale contrast, do not redesign actual height |

## 9. Ensemble / intentional resemblance

Default: **G. Ensemble / Shared-plane Framing + Waist-up + Eye-level + Front**
for a deliberately posed group. Preserve all important people, matching faces/
hair/shared gaze when requested, actual relative heights and prop counts.
Give every hand/object a clear owner. Do not blur the third person or force a
depth stagger; background detail follows the active scene contract.

## Assembly reminder

Use the main skill's single-frame order or the panel assembly contract.
Reuse resolved Style/Background Contracts and identity sentences consistently.
Requested wardrobe/expression changes belong in the active task Character Lock. A template
sets the scene; it does not grant permission to generate an image during a
prompt-only, QC-only or skill-update request.

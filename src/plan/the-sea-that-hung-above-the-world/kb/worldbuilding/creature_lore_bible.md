# 🐋 Creature & Disease Bible: The Sea That Hung Above The World

This document catalogs all mutated creatures, diseases, conditions, and ecological phenomena in the world. Every entry has a biological basis connected to the Sky Ocean ecology and a narrative purpose in the story. Nothing exists "just to be cool."

---

## PART 1: CREATURES (11 Species)

---

### 1. Cloud Whales (วาฬเมฆ)
- **Type**: Genetically altered cetacean — Mammal
- **Appearance**: 40-80 meters long. Grey-blue skin with bioluminescent scarring along old wounds. Fins disproportionately wide — adapted to "swim" through the dense air of the Sky Ocean. Iron hooks and metal fittings still embedded in their bones from when the Ascendant Engineers anchored them as supports for the Sky-Dam.
- **Behavior**: Migrate along Sky Ocean wind currents. Sing low-frequency songs that humans perceive as vibration. Repeat the same songs over centuries — believed to be "memories" of the ocean before it was lifted.
- **Origin/Lore**: Ancient humans captured Cloud Whales and used them as living pillars to support the Sky-Dam. The iron hooks in their bones are the remnants. When the Sky-Dam falls, the Cloud Whales descend with the ocean — swimming down through the Descent Zone.
- **First Appearance**: Arc 2 Ch 6 — Cael's first glimpse of the Sky Ocean. A pod of Cloud Whales migrating through the Shroud.
- **Last Appearance**: Arc 7 Ch 33 — Cloud Whales descending with the falling ocean.
- **Danger Level**: Low — Not aggressive. But collision with an airship is catastrophic.
- **Survival Method**: Yield right of way. Show respect. Do not make sudden noise.
- **Effect on Humans**: Their songs can trigger buried memories in brine-touched. Prolonged exposure without mutation causes Rain-Madness.
- **Narrative Use**: Atmospheric majesty. Omen — when Cloud Whales begin descending lower than usual, it means the ocean is about to fall.

### 2. Reef-Hounds (หมาปะการัง)
- **Type**: Mutated mammal — Canine origin
- **Appearance**: Wolf-like body, 1.2-1.5 meters at the shoulder. Skull has burst open — red coral grows through the bone, forming a jagged external helmet. Teeth are sharp coral fragments, constantly regenerating. Eyes are milky white — no iris. Paws leave salt-crust footprints.
- **Behavior**: Hunt in packs of 3-12. Communicate by rhythmically tapping the coral on their heads — a clicking language. Retain canine instincts — "guard doorways." Will circle the ruins of houses and sit beside broken doors, waiting for owners who will never return.
- **Origin/Lore**: Domestic dogs abandoned in flooded cities. Rain transformed them. The coral growth is the earth's memory of the seabed reclaiming their bodies.
- **First Appearance**: Arc 2 Ch 7 — Ambush on the *Albatross* deck among the drowned ruins.
- **Last Appearance**: Arc 7 Ch 31 — Final swarm during the Hallowmere evacuation, slaughtered by Varek.
- **Danger Level**: HIGH — One is manageable. A pack is a massacre.
- **Survival Method**: Never let them surround you. Target the lead hound — the pack's coordination breaks without it.
- **Effect on Humans**: Bites introduce coral spores. Wounds begin calcifying within hours if untreated.
- **Narrative Use**: Ambush tension. Chase scenes. Evacuation combat. Emotional horror — the dogs still guard houses with no houses left.

### 3. Pale-Moths (ผีเสื้อซีด)
- **Type**: Mutated insect — Lepidopteran origin
- **Appearance**: Wingspan of a human palm. Wings are translucent like lung tissue — blue bioluminescent veins visible through the membrane. Flight is completely silent. Body is a pale, powdered white — like ash that has not yet settled.
- **Behavior**: Land on the mouth or nose of the dying. Do not eat flesh — they consume the "final breath." Gather in swarms around battlefields and disaster sites hours before death occurs.
- **Origin/Lore**: Born from droplets of vaporized seawater falling on corpses. Folk belief says a Pale-Moth carries the memory of the dead person in its wings.
- **First Appearance**: Arc 1 Ch 4 — The village the Ministry burned. Pale-Moths swarm the pyre.
- **Last Appearance**: Arc 6 Ch 30 — Landing on Odran's lips as he drowns in Hallowmere's gate room.
- **Danger Level**: None to the living — But their presence is the most reliable death omen in the world.
- **Survival Method**: You cannot hide from them. If Pale-Moths land on you, you are already dying — you just don't know it yet.
- **Effect on Humans**: Psychological. Seeing a Pale-Moth land on someone you love is worse than watching them die.
- **Narrative Use**: Death omen. Visual horror. Emotional cue — when a Pale-Moth appears, someone is about to die. Used most powerfully on Odran's final scene.

### 4. Glass Eels (ปลาไหลแก้ว)
- **Type**: Parasite / Messenger organism
- **Appearance**: 5-15 cm long. Body completely transparent — internal organs visible as blue bioluminescent threads. Swim through water and through the bloodstream of brine-touched with equal ease.
- **Behavior**: Enter the human body through open wounds or by drinking seawater. Navigate through veins toward the heart. The closer they get to the heart, the clearer the host hears the sea's voice — specifically, the Leviathan's frequency.
- **Origin/Lore**: Unknown whether created by the Ascendant Engineers or emerged naturally from the Sky Ocean. Function as "transmitters" for the Leviathan's signal to brine-touched hosts.
- **First Appearance**: Arc 1 Ch 4 — In the bottled house Cael finds. Tiny Glass Eels swim inside the miniature rooms.
- **Last Appearance**: Arc 6 Ch 30 — Swimming past Odran's ankles in the flooding gate room.
- **Danger Level**: Not directly lethal — But they accelerate the Leviathan's call. Makes the host's mutation progress faster.
- **Survival Method**: Do not drink Sky Ocean water. Treat all wounds immediately with salt-free water.
- **Effect on Humans**: Increased Leviathan frequency reception. Accelerated brine-touch mutation. In late stages, hosts can "hear" other brine-touched across distances.
- **Narrative Use**: Body horror — eels visible in veins. Communication mechanism — Nio uses them to sense other brine-touched. Connection to Leviathan.

### 5. Keening Crabs (ปูคร่ำครวญ)
- **Type**: Mutated crustacean
- **Appearance**: 30-50 cm shell diameter. Shell is bone-white, textured like a human skull. Each crab has a sound-chamber on its shell — when vibrated, produces a sound indistinguishable from a crying child. Thousands together produce a chorus of weeping.
- **Behavior**: Congregate by the thousands on shipwrecks and drowned ruins. Their crying intensifies when humans approach. The frequency of crying matches the frequency of the last human to die in that location.
- **Origin/Lore**: Common shore crabs that fed on human remains in flooded areas. The crying sound is a side effect of chemical compounds in human tissue interacting with their shell chambers.
- **First Appearance**: Arc 4 Ch 20 — Bone Harbor at night. The entire harbor cries as the *Albatross* prepares to depart.
- **Last Appearance**: Arc 7 Ch 35 — New shoreline after the ocean falls. The crying is fainter now — fewer human remains to feed on.
- **Danger Level**: Low — don't bite. But the psychological pressure of their crying is immense.
- **Survival Method**: Ear protection. Move through quickly. Do not linger.
- **Effect on Humans**: Psychological. Prolonged exposure causes auditory hallucinations — hearing the voices of dead loved ones in the crab-song.
- **Narrative Use**: Sound horror. Atmospheric tension. The Bone Harbor departure scene — the crew silently preparing for the suicide mission while the harbor cries like children.

### 6. Hull-Barnacles (เพรียงกินเรือ)
- **Type**: Parasitic structure organism
- **Appearance**: 10-30 cm shell diameter. Black, glossy shells. Long white tendrils extend from the shell opening. Release green bioluminescent spores into the air. Sharp enough to cut rope and thin metal.
- **Behavior**: Attach to the underside of airships. Consume wood and metal — slowly. Release hallucinogenic spores that cause crew members to see things that aren't there.
- **Origin/Lore**: Evolved in the Sky Ocean where shipwrecks are abundant. The hallucinogenic spores are a defense mechanism — disoriented crew means the barnacles feed undisturbed.
- **First Appearance**: Arc 2 Ch 6 — Maera discovers something attached to the hull with "teeth."
- **Last Appearance**: Arc 5 Ch 21 — Maera scrapes off the last barnacles before the Sky-Dam approach.
- **Danger Level**: Medium — If untreated, the ship falls apart. Spores cause hallucinations.
- **Survival Method**: Regular hull scraping. Gas masks during scraping.
- **Effect on Humans**: Spore inhalation causes hallucinations — usually of the person you most fear losing. Maera sees Tovin dead. Ilyra sees her father's corpse ship.
- **Narrative Use**: Resource tension — the ship is deteriorating. Hallucination scenes — characters forced to confront their deepest fears. Reveals character through what they hallucinate.

### 7. Shade-Flame (แสงเงา)
- **Type**: Bio-luminescent phenomenon — Possibly sentient?
- **Appearance**: Soft blue-white lights floating in low mist. No fixed shape. Move slowly — like someone walking through shadow. When approached, they retreat. When ignored, they follow.
- **Behavior**: Appear in areas where brine-touched have died. Folk belief: they are "souls" or "residual memories." Scientists (Sera): chemical reaction between decomposition gases and the water memory of the Sky Ocean. The truth is deliberately ambiguous.
- **Origin/Lore**: Unknown. Perhaps nothing more than a chemical reaction. Perhaps exactly what the folk believe.
- **First Appearance**: Arc 1 Ch 4 — The burned village. Shade-Flames drifting through the smoke.
- **Last Appearance**: Arc 6 Ch 29 — Hallowmere. Shade-Flames gathering above the sacred lake as Bishop Corvein's followers drown.
- **Danger Level**: None. But they haunt the mind.
- **Survival Method**: Ignore them. They cannot harm you. They will not stop following until you leave the area.
- **Effect on Humans**: Psychological. People who see Shade-Flame report feeling "watched" for days afterward. Brine-touched can sometimes hear faint voices from them.
- **Narrative Use**: Atmospheric horror. Visual beauty masking dread. Ambiguity — is the supernatural real in this world, or is it just chemistry?

### 8. Spine Corals (ปะการังกระดูกสันหลัง)
- **Type**: Parasitic coral — Calcification organism
- **Appearance**: Pale white coral that attaches to open wounds. Grows along the spine, extracting calcium from vertebrae. When fully mature, bursts through the skin in a "bloom" of coral flowers. The host is fully conscious but completely immobile.
- **Behavior**: Infects brine-touched who stay too long in the Pearl Colony. The Warden of Pearl "cultivates" it — he considers the final coral bloom a form of beauty.
- **Origin/Lore**: A side effect of the Warden's "treatment" for brine-touched. He claims it preserves them. The reality: it turns them into living statues.
- **First Appearance**: Arc 4 Ch 16 — The Warden's "garden." Rows of human-shaped coral. Eyes still tracking movement.
- **Last Appearance**: Arc 4 Ch 17 — The team flees. The garden remains.
- **Danger Level**: Slow death — takes months. The host is conscious the entire time.
- **Survival Method**: Do not accept the Warden's "treatment."
- **Effect on Humans**: Complete immobilization. Consciousness preserved. Unable to speak, move, or close eyes. A living death.
- **Narrative Use**: Body horror centerpiece of Arc 4. The Warden's "garden" is the visual culmination of the theme: "nature reclaiming the human body is beautiful and horrifying simultaneously."

### 9. The Drowned Choir (คณะนักร้องใต้ทะเล)
- **Type**: Phenomenon — Possibly a collective organism? Possibly a natural echo effect?
- **Appearance**: No physical form. A chorus of hundreds of human voices singing the same song. Comes from beneath the surface of the Sky Ocean. The song changes depending on how many people have recently died in the water.
- **Behavior**: Grows louder when someone is dying in the Sky Ocean. Sings the same melody that the dying person last heard or sang in life. The song propagates through water — even through pipes and rain.
- **Origin/Lore**: Sera's theory: *"It is the collective echo of nervous systems still active in dead brine-touched bodies at the bottom of the Sky Ocean. They sing what they remember."* The folk belief: *"The dead are singing to call the living home."*
- **First Appearance**: Arc 2 Ch 7 — Faintly, as the *Albatross* sails over a drowned city.
- **Last Appearance**: Arc 7 Ch 33 — Loudest. The Sky-Dam has collapsed. Thousands are dying. The Choir is deafening.
- **Danger Level**: Listening too long causes dissociation — not an attack, but a "perception" the human mind cannot withstand.
- **Survival Method**: Do not listen. Plug ears. Sing a different song — it confuses the Choir momentarily.
- **Effect on Humans**: Prolonged listening causes temporary loss of self — the listener begins to "remember" lives that are not their own.
- **Narrative Use**: Sound horror. Thematic resonance — all the dead have a "voice." The Choir is the cumulative sound of all the world's drowned.

### 10. The First Leviathan (มังกรทะเลแรก)
- **Type**: Unclassifiable — Possibly an animal. Possibly a nervous system. Possibly a god. Possibly the wound of the world given sentience.
- **Appearance**: No one has seen it in its entirety. Those who have glimpsed parts describe: *"Like seeing the brain of God… or the boundary where the world connects to something else."* Nio — the only one who has truly seen it — cannot describe it in human language. He can only transmit whale frequencies of the experience.
- **Behavior**: Does not "do" anything. It "is." It is the total perception of the entire ancient ocean — every death, every birth, every current, every memory of the seabed. Its existence makes all sea-life in the Sky Ocean begin to "remember." It does not want the ocean to fall. It does not hate humanity. It simply feels the pain of the ocean being torn from the earth, and that feeling is loud enough to drive humans insane.
- **Origin/Lore**: May be the first living organism of the primordial ocean, lifted into the sky when the ocean was raised 1,000 years ago. Or it may be a "product" of the ocean being torn — the wound itself, alive.
- **First Appearance (indirect)**: Arc 2 Ch 6 — Cael feels "a silence that isn't silence" beneath the waves.
- **First Appearance (direct)**: Arc 5 Ch 25 — Nio sees it fully. His human language begins to fail.
- **Last Appearance**: Arc 7 Ch 34 — Returns to the sea as the ocean falls. Disperses — its consciousness spreads across the new ocean, no longer concentrated in one crushing point.
- **Danger Level**: Beyond measurement — Does not "attack," but humans who look at it directly "understand" the universe too completely.
- **Survival Method**: Do not look. Do not listen. Do not attempt to understand. Human consciousness cannot contain what the Leviathan perceives.
- **Effect on Humans**: Direct sight → irreversible perceptual overload. The viewer understands the entire ecological system — every death, every current, every memory — and the human mind breaks trying to contain it.
- **Narrative Use**: Climax. Cael must "negotiate" with it through Nio. It is not an antagonist — it is a natural force that must be "released" back to the earth. The Leviathan is the ultimate expression of the theme: nature is not evil, but it is not gentle.

### 10-A. The Choir-Warden (ผู้พิทักษ์คณะนักร้อง) — Layer 3-4 / Sky-Dam Threshold

> **Design note (horror principle — Iron Lung film lineage):** The Choir-Warden is built on the horror architecture of the *Iron Lung* film (Markiplier, 2026), not the game. The film's monster is not silent — it *speaks*, through the voice of a dead woman, and the voice is the horror, because it means the monster has *absorbed* someone and is using them to communicate. The film's protagonist *hallucinates* — blood seeps into the submarine, and the hallucinations become indistinguishable from reality, and the audience cannot tell what is real. The film's climax is a *merge* — Simon does not defeat the monster; he merges with a seed and becomes a tree that kills it, and the merge is body horror: his body mutates, grows, stops being human. And above all of this, there is a *cosmic entity* — a giant eye in a red sky, watching, that is larger than the monster, larger than the ocean, larger than anything the protagonist can comprehend. The Choir-Warden inherits all four elements: it *speaks* through the voices of the dead it has absorbed; its psychic field causes *hallucinations* that the crew cannot distinguish from reality; the climax of the encounter is Nio *merging* with it — his body mutating, accelerating toward the Silence, becoming less human in real time; and behind the Warden, sensed but never seen, is the *First Leviathan* — the cosmic entity that is the ocean's awareness, watching through the Warden the way the eye in the red sky watched Simon. The Warden is not the final horror. The Warden is the *lens* through which the crew glimpses the final horror — the Leviathan, which is too vast to look at directly, and the Warden is the thing that makes them look.
>
> **The three encounters:** The Warden is encountered three times. The first is a *glimpse* — barely a sighting, something that could be a sensor error, a whale, a reef. The crew almost misses it. The reader, if paying attention, will not. The second is the *confrontation* — the crew enters the Warden's territory and the Warden enters theirs. This is the hallucination, the psychic pressure, the moment where the crew cannot tell what is real. The third is the *passage* — the crew must go through the Warden to reach the Dead Parliament. The Warden does not let them pass. It *tests* them. The test is not combat. The test is *being seen* — and *being changed*.

- **Type**: Unclassifiable — a massive, ancient marine organism that is *also* a psychic phenomenon. Sera's best classification, written in her journal with hands that would not stop shaking: *"It is not an animal. It is not a god. It is the ocean's immune system, and it has decided we are an infection."* The crew calls it "the Warden" because that is the word the Dead Parliament uses — but the Parliament did not build it. The Parliament *found* it. It was already there, in the deep, in the Layer 3-4 boundary, circling the Sky-Dam's foundations like a shark circling a wreck. The Parliament learned to *direct* it — to point it at intruders, to use it as a gatekeeper — but they never controlled it, and they never understood what it was, and some of them, in the private archives Cael reads in Arc 5, admitted this: *"We are using a thing we do not understand to guard a thing we cannot repair. This is not strategy. This is prayer."*

- **Size and Form**: The Warden is *large*. How large, the crew never determines — and that is part of the horror. The *Albatross* is 40 meters long. The Warden, in the sonar returns that Maera can barely interpret, is *bigger than the ship*. Not twice as big. Not three times. The sonar cannot find the edges of it. The returns come back wrong — too dense, too deep, as if the thing the sonar is bouncing off is not a solid object but a *density in the water*, a place where the ocean is thicker. Maera's first reading: *"That's not a creature. That's a... a region. A part of the ocean that's... aware."* She is not wrong. The Warden is not a thing in the water. It is a *quality* of the water — a region where the ocean's awareness is concentrated, given form by a body that is mostly water, mostly salt, mostly memory. The body, what little of it the crew sees, is *wrong* in the way deep-sea creatures are always wrong: it was not built for human eyes. It was not built for light. Parts of it glow — bioluminescent patterns that pulse in rhythms that match the Drowned Choir, as if the body is *singing to itself*. The parts that glow are the only parts the crew ever sees clearly, and they are the worst parts, because they are *faces* — not human faces, not fish faces, but something in between, something that suggests a face without being one, the way a shadow suggests a person without being one. The crew cannot count them. They move. They multiply. They open and close like eyes, like mouths, like wounds.

- **The Voice (the Warden speaks — Iron Lung film element)**: The Warden is not silent. This is the horror that elevates it from creature to *presence*. It speaks — not with its own voice, because it does not have one. It speaks with the voices of the dead it has absorbed. A thousand years of drowned sailors, Salt Ward children, brine-touched who transformed and were consumed — their voices are the Warden's vocabulary. It does not choose which voice to use. The voice that comes through is the one most *relevant* to the person hearing it. Tovin hears his mother — a woman who died when he was eleven, whose voice he has not heard in eight years, speaking words she never said in life: *"Don't be afraid, Tovin. The water is not cold. The water is not cold. The water is not cold."* The repetition is the horror. The voice is real — it is his mother's voice, captured from her death (she drowned in a flood when Tovin was eleven), preserved in the Warden's memory, and played back to her son as a *lure*, or a *greeting*, or a *warning* — the crew cannot tell which, and the not-knowing is worse than the voice itself. Cassian hears a voice he does not recognize at first — a child's voice, calm, saying: *"You chose to save me."* It is Reven's voice. Reven, who Cassian has not yet encountered (that happens in Arc 5 Ch 23). The Warden is speaking to Cassian in the voice of a child he has not yet failed — a voice from his *future*, pulled from the Warden's perception of what is coming. This is the moment Cassian understands that the Warden does not only remember the past. It perceives the *future*. And it is showing Cassian what he will do, in the voice of the person he will do it to. Cael hears Nio. Not the Nio standing behind him — the Nio of ten years ago, the four-year-old, saying: *"พี่, ฝนตก."* Brother, it's raining. The voice is warm. The voice is real. Cael turns, expecting to see his brother as a child, and there is nothing but dark water. The Warden has found the voice that will break Cael's composure, and it is using it. Not as an attack. As an *introduction*. The Warden is telling Cael: *I know who you are. I know who you love. I have them. They are part of me now. And soon, so will you be.*

- **The Eye Behind (the cosmic entity — Iron Lung film element)**: The Warden is not the final horror. Behind the Warden — above it, around it, *through* it — is the First Leviathan. The crew does not see the Leviathan in Encounter 2. They *feel* it. The psychic field that bends their minds is not the Warden's alone — it is the Warden acting as a *lens* for the Leviathan's perception, the way a magnifying glass focuses sunlight. The Warden is vast. The Leviathan is *vastness*. When Nio, in Encounter 3, touches the Warden, he does not touch the Warden. He touches the Leviathan *through* the Warden — and for one moment, he sees the Eye. Not the Warden's eye. The Leviathan's. A single point of awareness so large that it fills the entire Sky Ocean, looking at him, looking *through* him, looking at everything he has ever been and will ever be. Nio does not scream. He goes silent. His human language fails — not gradually, as the Silence has been failing, but *all at once*, for thirty seconds, during which he is not Nio. He is a point of perception in the Leviathan's awareness, and the Leviathan is looking at itself through him. When Nio comes back, he is crying, and he cannot say why, and the first word he says is: *"ใหญ่."* Big. That is all he can manage. The crew does not understand. The reader understands: Nio has seen the thing behind the Warden, and it is the Leviathan, and the Leviathan is the size of the ocean, and the ocean is looking at him.

- **The Hallucinations (Iron Lung film element — reality breaks)**: The Warden's psychic field does not just *pressure* the crew. It *replaces* their reality. This is the Iron Lung film horror: the protagonist cannot tell what is real. The crew of the *Albatross* experiences the same dissolution. During Encounter 2, the boundary between what is happening and what the Warden is *showing* them dissolves. Maera is in the engine room. She checks the pressure gauge. It reads normal. She looks away. She looks back. The gauge reads a depth that is impossible — 12,000 meters, the bottom of the Sky Ocean, a depth that would crush the *Albatross* instantly. She checks the hull. The hull is intact. She checks the gauge again. Normal. She cannot tell which reading was real. This is the horror: not that the Warden shows her false things, but that it makes *both* readings feel equally real, and she cannot determine which reality she is in. Tovin, on deck, sees the *Albatross* from the outside — as if he is standing on the water, looking at the ship. He can see himself on the deck, looking out at the water. He is in two places at once. He does not know which one is real. He pinches his arm. He is still in both places. The hallucination does not break when he tests it. It breaks when Cassian grabs his shoulder and says his name — and even then, Tovin is not sure the Cassian holding him is the real Cassian. He is not sure for the rest of the arc. The reader is not sure either. This is the design: during the Warden encounters, the prose itself should become *unreliable*. The reader should not be able to distinguish hallucination from event. The crew cannot. The reader should share their uncertainty.

- **The Merge (Iron Lung film element — body horror climax)**: In the Iron Lung film, Simon does not defeat the monster. He *merges* with a seed, and his body mutates into a tree that kills the monster. The merge is the climax, and it is body horror: his body stops being human in real time. Nio's passage through the Warden is the same beat. Nio does not simply *pass through* the Warden. He *merges* with it — briefly, partially, irreversibly. When the Warden's attention focuses on Nio, his body begins to change. Not the slow transformation of the Brine-Spore. A *rapid* acceleration. Coral growths erupt along his forearms — visible, audible, the sound of bone calcifying in real time. His skin, already translucent, becomes *transparent* — the crew can see his veins, can see the Glass Eels moving inside them, can see the coral structures forming along his ribs. His eyes change — the pupils dilate, then contract to vertical slits, then dilate again, as if his eyes are trying to decide what kind of eyes they are. He opens his mouth to speak, and what comes out is not a word. It is a frequency. The crew feels it in their chests. Nio is *singing* — not human singing, whale singing, the frequency of the Leviathan, and the frequency is coming from inside his chest, from the coral that is growing there, and the coral is *singing*. This is the merge. Nio does not become a tree. He becomes a *reef* — a small, human-shaped reef, standing in the corridor, singing the Leviathan's song, and the song is what parts the Warden. The Warden does not part because Nio is brine-touched. It parts because Nio has become *part of the ocean's music* — a living instrument, a tuning fork that the Warden recognizes as its own frequency. The crew follows Nio through. They do not look at him. They cannot look at him. The coral on his arms is still growing. By the time they reach the Regulator Chamber, Nio has lost the word for "water." He does not notice. Cael notices. Cael says nothing. There is nothing to say. The merge is not reversible. The Warden has taken its price. Nio has paid.

- **Encounter 1 — The Glimpse (Arc 2 Ch 7, Sunken Quarter)**: The *Albatross* is drifting over the drowned ruins. Ilyra is at the helm. The sonar is pinging the buildings below — standard navigation. One ping comes back *wrong*. The return is too large, too deep, and it moves — not with the current, but against it, as if something down there is swimming. Maera checks the sensor. It is working. She pings again. The return is gone. There is nothing there but the buildings. She logs it as an anomaly — "possible whale, possible reef structure, sensor ghost" — and moves on. She does not mention it to the crew. But Cael, standing on the deck, feels something he cannot name. A pressure behind his eyes. A feeling of being *noticed*. He looks over the rail, into the dark water, and for a moment — less than a moment, a fraction of a second — he sees a light below the surface. Not a Lure-Lantern. Not a bioluminescent fish. A *pattern* — a pulse, like a heartbeat, like something breathing. Then it is gone. He tells no one. He writes it in his rain-cord memory that night: a new knot, a new pattern, for a thing he cannot explain. The reader, if paying attention, will notice that this is the first appearance of the Warden. The crew does not know. The crew will not know until Arc 5. The reader may not know until Arc 5 either. But the knot is there. The Warden has noticed them. It will not forget.

- **Encounter 2 — The Confrontation (Arc 5 Ch 21, Layer 3 descent)**: The *Albatross* descends into the Pressure Veil to approach the Sky-Dam's underwater foundations. The water goes dark. The pressure rises. Maera has reinforced the hull, but the hull is not rated for this depth, and every rivet groans. Then the psychic field hits. It does not announce itself. It is simply *there*, the way the pressure is there — a fact of the environment, not an event. And then the voices begin. Not the Drowned Choir — that has been with them since Arc 2. *Individual voices*. Voices the crew recognizes. Tovin hears his mother — a woman who drowned when he was eleven — saying, in the calm voice she used when she was trying to keep him from panicking: *"Don't be afraid, Tovin. The water is not cold. The water is not cold. The water is not cold."* The repetition is the horror. The voice is real. It is her voice, captured from her death, preserved in the Warden's memory. Tovin turns. No one is there. He turns back. The voice is still there. Cassian hears a child's voice he does not recognize yet — calm, saying: *"You chose to save me."* It is Reven's voice. Reven, whom Cassian has not yet encountered. The Warden is speaking to Cassian in the voice of a child he has not yet failed — a voice from his *future*. Cael hears Nio — not the Nio behind him, but the Nio of ten years ago, the four-year-old, saying: *"พี่, ฝนตก."* Brother, it's raining. Cael turns, expecting to see his brother as a child. Dark water. Nothing else. Then the hallucinations begin. Maera checks the pressure gauge. Normal. She looks away. She looks back. The gauge reads 12,000 meters — a depth that would crush the ship instantly. She checks the hull. Intact. She checks the gauge. Normal. She cannot tell which reading was real. Tovin, on deck, sees the *Albatross* from the outside — as if he is standing on the water, looking at the ship, looking at himself on the deck. He is in two places at once. He pinches his arm. He is still in both places. The hallucination does not break when he tests it. It breaks when Cassian grabs his shoulder — and even then, Tovin is not sure the Cassian holding him is real. He is not sure for the rest of the arc. The reader is not sure either. This is the design: during the Warden encounters, the prose itself becomes *unreliable*. Cassian, at the helm, feels his hands go cold — the cold of *being seen*. He says: *"Hold course. Hold silence. Whatever you are hearing, whatever you are seeing — it is not real. Do not react."* He is wrong. It is real. It is just not happening *to* them — it is happening *through* them. Then the sonar returns something. Maera reads it. Her face goes white. *"Cassian."* The screen shows a shape below them — larger than the ship, larger than anything they have seen, circling slowly. Cassian watches it for thirty seconds. *"That's not hunting us. That's *evaluating* us. There's a difference."* The shape circles for eleven minutes. The voices do not stop. The hallucinations do not stop. Tovin is weeping silently, his mother's voice still in his ears. Maera's knuckles are white on the console. Ilyra's breathing is wrong — too fast, too shallow. Then the shape descends below sonar range. The voices fade — slowly, like a tide going out. The hallucinations thin, but do not vanish entirely. For the rest of the arc, the crew will occasionally see things that are not there — a face in a porthole, a voice in the engine noise, a pressure gauge that reads wrong for one glance and right the next. They do not know if this is the Warden still perceiving them, or if the Warden has *changed* them — left a residue in their perception that will not wash out. When Cassian finally speaks, his voice is flat: *"We're going to have to go through it to reach the Dam."* No one answers. They all know he is right. They all know what "through it" means. They do not know if they will survive it. The reader does not know either.

- **Encounter 3 — The Passage (Arc 5 Ch 22, Sky-Dam corridor)**: The corridor leading to the Regulator Chamber. The *Albatross* is too large to enter — the crew proceeds on foot, in pressure suits, through a flooded service tunnel. The tunnel is dark. The lights on their suits cut cones of visibility that reach maybe ten meters. Beyond the light, nothing. The water is warm — body temperature, the warmth of a living thing pressed against the skin. This is the first wrongness. The water should be cold at this depth. It is not. It is *warm*, as if something in the tunnel is generating heat, is *alive*, is breathing. The Drowned Choir, which the crew has heard as distant singing for three arcs, is no longer distant. It is *in the walls*. It is in the water. It is in their teeth. The sound is not a sound — it is a vibration, a pressure, a sensation of being *asked something* that bypasses the ears and lands behind the sternum. The crew does not understand the question. They understand only that they are being *asked*. Then the lights begin to fail. Not all at once — one by one, the suit lights flicker and die, as if something is *draining* them, as if the Warden's psychic field is interfering with the electronics. The crew is in darkness. In the darkness, the Warden appears. Not as a shape — as a *presence*. The water around them thickens. It does not freeze. It does not solidify. It becomes *aware* — the water itself is aware, as if the ocean in this corridor has become part of the Warden's body, and the crew is inside it, and the Warden is *feeling* them the way a tongue feels food, slowly, curiously, without malice. The crew cannot move. Not because they are restrained — because their bodies have stopped responding to their minds. The Warden's psychic field, at this intensity, *overrides* voluntary movement. The crew is conscious. They can breathe. They can think. They cannot move. This is the body horror: not that the Warden changes their bodies, but that it *takes them* — that their bodies become, briefly, part of the Warden's perception, and the Warden uses them to *look at themselves*, the way you might use a mirror, and the crew can feel it happening, can feel their hands being moved by something that is not them, can feel their eyes being turned toward faces in the water that they do not want to see. Odran, in this moment, is touched by the Warden's full attention — and the Warden, perceiving Odran, perceives *what Odran has done*. The 73 children. The Warden does not judge. It does not punish. It *shows* — it shows Odran every death, every child, every moment of the Salt Ward, not as punishment but as *acknowledgment*, as if the Warden has been holding these deaths in its memory for years, waiting for someone to come who could *receive* them, and Odran is that someone, not because he is guilty but because he is *present*, and the Warden does not distinguish between guilt and presence. It simply gives him the deaths. All of them. At once. Odran does not scream. He does not move. When it ends, he sits on the floor of the tunnel and does not speak for an hour. When he speaks, the first thing he says is: *"They weren't punishing me. They were... giving them to me. As if I needed to carry them. As if they had been waiting for someone to take them."* The horror is not the pain. The horror is the *gift* — the dead have been waiting a thousand years for someone to *receive* them, and the Warden does not care whether the receiver is guilty or innocent, only that they are *here*.

- **Why Nio Passes (The Merge — body horror climax)**: Nio steps forward. He does not know why. He is not brave — he is *called*. The Warden's attention shifts from the crew to Nio. The psychic pressure, which has been crushing everyone equally, *changes* — it becomes, for Nio, something else. Not lighter. Not heavier. *Different*. The Warden recognizes the Brine-Spore in his blood. The same parasite. The same transformation. Nio is not a threat. Nio is *part of the system*. And then the merge begins. Nio's body starts to change — not the slow transformation of the Brine-Spore, but a *rapid* acceleration. Coral growths erupt along his forearms — visible, audible, the sound of bone calcifying in real time. His skin, already translucent, becomes *transparent* — the crew can see his veins, can see the Glass Eels moving inside them, can see the coral structures forming along his ribs. His eyes change — pupils dilate, contract to vertical slits, dilate again, as if his eyes cannot decide what kind of eyes they are. He opens his mouth to speak, and what comes out is not a word. It is a frequency. The crew feels it in their chests. Nio is *singing* — not human singing, whale singing, the frequency of the Leviathan, and the frequency is coming from inside his chest, from the coral that is growing there, and the coral is *singing*. This is the merge. Nio does not become a tree, like Simon in Iron Lung. He becomes a *reef* — a small, human-shaped reef, standing in the corridor, singing the Leviathan's song. And in that moment, Nio sees the Eye. Not the Warden's eye. The Leviathan's. A single point of awareness so large that it fills the entire Sky Ocean, looking at him, looking *through* him, looking at everything he has ever been and will ever be. Nio does not scream. He goes silent — not the gradual Silence that has been taking his words, but a total silence, for thirty seconds, during which he is not Nio. He is a point of perception in the Leviathan's awareness, and the Leviathan is looking at itself through him. When Nio comes back, he is crying, and he cannot say why, and the first word he says is: *"ใหญ่."* Big. That is all he can manage. The song — the frequency coming from the coral in his chest — is what parts the Warden. The Warden does not part because Nio is brine-touched. It parts because Nio has become *part of the ocean's music* — a living instrument, a tuning fork that the Warden recognizes as its own frequency. The water does not part — it *thins*, becoming less aware, as if the Warden has withdrawn its attention to listen to the song. The crew follows Nio through. They do not look at him. They cannot look at him. The coral on his arms is still growing. By the time they reach the Regulator Chamber, Nio has lost the word for "water." He does not notice. Cael notices. Cael says nothing. There is nothing to say. The merge is not reversible. The Warden has taken its price. Nio has paid. And behind them, in the corridor, the Warden is still singing — but now it is singing Nio's song, the frequency he gave it, and the frequency is the Leviathan's, and the Leviathan is listening, and the Leviathan is pleased.

- **What the Crew Does Not Learn**: The crew never learns the Warden's full size. They never learn whether it is a single organism or a colony. They never learn whether the faces in the water are the actual consciousnesses of the dead, or the Warden's *imitation* of them, or something else that has no name. They never learn whether the Warden could have stopped them — whether it *chose* to let them pass, or whether Nio's presence simply made them *invisible* to it, the way a river does not notice a fish. They never learn whether the Warden is alive, in any sense a human would recognize. Sera writes three theories in her journal and crosses out all of them. In the final chronicle, she writes: *"We passed through the Warden. I do not know what it was. I do not know if it let us pass or if it could not see us. I am not certain it is gone. On rainy nights, I still feel it — a pressure behind my eyes, a feeling of being noticed. I do not write this in the chronicle. I am writing it here because I need someone to know, even if that someone is only me."*

- **Last Appearance**: Arc 5 Ch 22. The Warden does not appear again. When the Sky-Dam collapses, something is released — the crew feels it, a vast exhalation, a sound like a thousand voices finally allowed to stop singing — but whether this is the Warden dying, or being freed, or simply *moving* to somewhere else in the deep, no one knows. In the Epilogue, on rainy nights, Sera sometimes feels the pressure behind her eyes again — faint, distant, like a memory of being noticed. She does not tell anyone. She is not sure she wants to know if it is still there.

- **Danger Level**: BEYOND MEASUREMENT. The Warden does not attack. It does not need to. Its presence is the danger — the psychic field that bends minds, the perception that *shows* things no human should see, the weight of a thousand years of accumulated death that it *gives* to anyone who enters its territory. You cannot fight it. You cannot flee it. You can only endure it, and hope that what is left of you when you come out the other side is still *you*.

- **Survival Method**: Unknown. The crew survives because Nio is brine-touched and the Warden's attention *integrates* with him rather than crushing him. Whether this would work for any brine-touched, or only for Nio, or only for that moment, is never established. Loran's charts, written before he leaves, mark the entire Sky-Dam approach with a single word: *"PRAY."* Cassian, after the encounter, adds a line beneath it in his own hand: *"Praying didn't help. Nio did."*

- **Narrative Use**: The Choir-Warden is the story's *final guardian* — the last thing between the crew and the Dead Parliament, and the most terrifying thing in the Sky Ocean, because it cannot be fought, cannot be fled, and cannot be understood. It is the ocean's immune response, given form and awareness, and it has decided that the crew is an infection — or it has decided nothing, and is simply *being*, and its being is incompatible with human sanity. The horror is Iron Lung horror: the crew is trapped in a metal box in the dark, and something vast is outside, and they cannot see it, and it can see them, and it is *in their minds*, and there is nothing they can do except keep going and hope they survive the passage. The Warden is not the final boss. The Dead Parliament is the final boss — the thing that made the decision, that chose the lie, that built the system. The Warden is the thing that *guards* the final boss — not out of loyalty, not out of malice, but because it is the wound the final boss created, and the wound has become a living thing, and the living thing will not let anyone reach the one who made it without first *showing them what was done*. The Warden is the cost of the Dead Parliament's choice, made flesh, made water, made aware. And it has been waiting a thousand years for someone to come who could *hold* what it carries.

## PART 2: PARASITES & CONDITIONS (ปรสิตและสภาวะ)

---

### 1. Brine-Spores (สปอร์น้ำเค็ม) — The Core Parasite
- **Type**: Parasitic microorganism — Spore-form
- **Life Cycle**: Brine-Spores are carried in Sky Ocean rain. On contact with human skin, the spore burrows through the epidermis within minutes — the entry point becomes translucent as the spore dissolves the skin's pigment layer to access the bloodstream. Once in the blood, it migrates toward the nervous system, following bioelectric signals toward the brainstem. The migration takes weeks to months (Stage 1-2). Upon reaching the central nervous system, the spore begins integrating with the host's neural architecture — this is the point of no return (Stage 3). The parasite does not kill the host. It transforms the host into a hybrid organism capable of surviving in the sea. Nio is still Nio — he just experiences reality through a biology that is no longer exclusively human.
- **Early Stage Symptoms (Week 1-4)**: Entry point becomes translucent — veins and muscle visible beneath. Cold sensation that never fades. The parasite is consuming pigment cells to build its own protective membrane.
- **Mid Stage Symptoms (Month 2-6)**: Pale scales form — the parasite constructs external armor from the host's mineral intake. Bones soften or harden — the parasite restructures the skeleton for pressure adaptation. Constant water sound in the head — the parasite has reached the inner ear, calibrating hearing to Leviathan frequencies.
- **Late Stage Symptoms (Month 6+)**: Full semi-aquatic transformation — functional gills (parasite-constructed respiratory tissue), coral-reinforced bones, webbing, fins. Speech degrades into whale frequencies — the parasite has rewired the language centers. The parasite coexists with the host's consciousness — the host is still themselves, just experiencing reality through a new biology.
- **First Mention**: Arc 1 Ch 1 — Folk tales describe "the shimmer in the rain."
- **First Appearance**: Arc 1 Ch 2 (Flashback) — Nio's fingers turn translucent. Under magnification: microscopic spore entry wounds.
- **Last Appearance**: Arc 7 Ch 33 — Nio and Varek complete transformation.
- **Plot Impact**: Central conflict driver. The reason the Ministry exists (containment). The reason Cael searches for Nio. The reason Varek hunts and later defects.
- **Character Impact**: Cael (fear of his own infection), Nio (10-year host), Varek (hiding infection), Odran (guilt over sending children to be "studied").
- **Narrative Use**: Body horror of parasitic invasion. Thematic question: if a parasite transforms you into something that can survive — is it a disease or an adaptation?

---

### 2. Ash-Lung (ปอดเถ้า) — Environmental Condition
- **Type**: Environmental — not parasitic. Long-term inhalation of airborne ash and vaporized sea minerals from Ministry burn-sites and Sky Ocean spray.
- **Symptoms**: Audible wet rattle in the chest when breathing. Coughing up grey-flecked phlegm. Lungs slowly fill with fluid — the patient drowns on dry land.
- **First Appearance**: Arc 3 Ch 13 — Capital workers in the steam-pipe corridors. Odran hears the "water in their lungs" as they pass.
- **Narrative Use**: Background horror. Odran's guilt trigger — he hears Ash-Lung and remembers the Salt Ward. A reminder that even those not directly touched by rain are dying from the Sky Ocean.

---

### 3. Coral-Spores (สปอร์ปะการัง) — Parasitic Coral
- **Type**: Parasitic coral spore — Calcification organism
- **Life Cycle**: Coral-Spores enter through open wounds or are deliberately introduced (the Warden's "treatment"). The spore attaches to the nearest bone and begins extracting calcium, consuming the host's skeleton cell by cell and rebuilding it as living coral. The process takes months. The host is conscious throughout. The parasite preserves the nervous system — the host's bioelectric field nourishes the coral. Fully matured colonies bloom from the host's body like flowering coral. The Warden considers this the highest form of beauty.
- **Symptoms**: Small coral buds break through skin at fingertips. Spread inward along bone lines toward the spine. Terminal stage: entire skeleton replaced by coral. Host immobile but fully conscious. Eyes track movement. The colony pulses faintly — bioluminescent in the dark.
- **Origin**: Naturally occurring in Pearl Colony's salt-saturated environment. The Warden cultivates and refines the spores.
- **First Appearance**: Arc 4 Ch 16 — Pearl Colony. The Warden's "garden" — rows of Coral-Spore hosts in full bloom.
- **Narrative Use**: Body horror centerpiece of Arc 4. The "living statues." The parasite makes the host "beautiful" — and that IS the horror.

---

### 4. The Silence (ความเงียบ)
- **Type**: Neurological condition — Late-stage Brine-Spore infection
- **Cause**: Brine-Spore hosts who have connected with the Leviathan for too long.
- **Symptoms**: Gradual loss of ability to hear human speech. Can only hear the frequencies of the sea. Cannot speak human language — can only produce whale sounds. Memory loss — human words disappear one by one. The first word lost is always the name of the person they love most.
- **First Appearance**: Arc 5 Ch 25 — Nio begins losing language. Forgets the word "water." Can still say "brother."
- **Narrative Use**: The emotional core of Nio's tragedy. His final scene: he has forgotten the word "brother" but sings Cael's childhood melody in whale frequency. Cael understands. The loss of language is the loss of the last thread connecting Nio to his human life.

---

### 5. The Warden's Bloom (ดอกวอร์เดน) — Parasitic Calcification Agent
- **Type**: Parasitic organism — Full-tissue calcification agent, cultivated by the Warden of Pearl
- **Life Cycle**: The Warden's Bloom is an engineered parasite administered via injection or salve to brine-touched who come seeking his "mercy." Once in the bloodstream, it converts all soft tissue — muscle, skin, organs — into a unified calcium-carbonate structure over weeks to months. The parasite feeds on the host's metabolic energy to fuel the calcification. Unlike Coral-Spores (which replace bone), the Warden's Bloom converts EVERYTHING into a single living statue. The nervous system is conserved — the host is conscious, trapped in an immobile body, for years. Some hosts in the Warden's garden are decades old. Their eyes still follow visitors.
- **Symptoms**: Body hardens from feet upward. Skin becomes marble-white, smooth, cool. Cannot move, speak, or close eyes. Consciousness preserved.
- **Origin**: The Warden discovered the organism in the Pearl Colony lake. He cultivates it. He calls it a "blessing of preservation." It is not.
- **First Appearance**: Arc 4 Ch 16 — Pearl Colony. The Warden's "garden."
- **Narrative Use**: The full horror of "preservation" as violation. The parasite does not kill — it collects.

---

### 6. Rain-Madness (บ้าฝน)
- **Type**: Psychological condition
- **Cause**: Non-mutated humans who hear the Leviathan's call — typically occurs in Rain Listeners who refuse their role. The Leviathan's frequency is too loud for an unmutated mind.
- **Symptoms**: Hallucinations of the ocean in dry places. Speaking a language no one understands. Compulsion to walk toward the Sky Ocean. Terminal stage: the afflicted walks into the Sky Ocean and does not return.
- **First Appearance**: Arc 4 Ch 17 — Kaelen Mor. The Rain Listener who refused. Half-coral, half-human, still conscious. *"I ran… but the sea doesn't forget. It just waits."*
- **Narrative Use**: Shows the cost of refusing the calling. Makes Cael's choice more desperate — the alternatives are madness (Kaelen) or sacrifice (Tide-Key). There is no third option.

## PART 3: ECOLOGICAL PHENOMENA (5 Types)

---

### 1. Bitter Rain (ฝนขม)
- **Description**: Rain with high salinity. When it falls on soil, normal plants die within days. Only hardy vegetation survives.
- **Ecological Impact**: Agricultural land shrinks year by year. Cities dependent on "rain gardens" — hydroponic farms growing tolerant crops. Food scarcity is a constant background pressure.
- **First Appearance**: Arc 1 Ch 2 (Flashback) — The rain Nio touches.
- **Plot Use**: Economic pressure driving cities toward dependence on Sky-Dam energy. Background worldbuilding — the rain is slowly starving everyone, not just mutating the unlucky.

---

### 2. The Shroud (ม่านหมอก)
- **Description**: A dense fog layer between the earth's surface and the Sky Ocean. Thickness varies — at its densest, visibility is zero. Airships must use high speed to "punch through." Temperature inside the Shroud drops below freezing. Strange sounds echo in the fog — possibly the Leviathan, possibly the Drowned Choir, possibly just wind.
- **Ecological Impact**: The physical barrier separating the world below from the world above. Makes Sky Ocean travel dangerous and expensive.
- **First Appearance**: Arc 1 Ch 5 — The *Albatross* ascends through it.
- **Plot Use**: Transition between worlds. Danger during ascent/descent. Isolation — once inside the Shroud, you are truly cut off from both earth and sky.

---

### 3. Ash-Blooms (ดอกเถ้าบาน)
- **Description**: Phenomenon where seawater vapor on the Sky Ocean's surface crystallizes into flower-like formations. Beautiful — intricate pale structures that refract light into rainbow patterns. Release toxic gas on contact with air.
- **Ecological Impact**: Blooms kill birds that fly too low. Ships that sail through them suffer crew poisoning.
- **First Appearance**: Arc 2 Ch 7 — The Sky Ocean. Ilyra steers around an Ash-Bloom field.
- **Plot Use**: Visual beauty masking danger — a microcosm of the story's theme. Environmental obstacle.

---

### 4. Tide-Singing (เสียงกระแสน้ำ)
- **Description**: Phenomenon where water in pipes, rivers, and drainage systems begins to "sing" at the Leviathan's frequency. Grows louder as the Sky-Dam weakens. Heard even in cities far from the Sky Ocean — the Leviathan's call propagates through all connected water.
- **Ecological Impact**: Psychological — people who hear Tide-Singing report nightmares and feelings of being "called" somewhere.
- **First Appearance**: Arc 2 Ch 7 — Faintly. Arc 3 Ch 11 — Sera hears it in the archive pipes.
- **Plot Use**: Mystery seed. Signal that the Leviathan is waking. Countdown mechanism — the louder the Tide-Singing, the closer the Sky-Dam is to collapse.

---

### 5. The Descent Zone (เขตทะเลตก)
- **Description**: Areas where water from the Sky Ocean begins to "leak" downward as the Sky-Dam fails. Appears as dense cold fog. Temperature drops. Sky Ocean creatures — small fish, Pale-Moths, Glass Eels — fall through with the water.
- **Ecological Impact**: Death zone for anything beneath it. Soil becomes saturated with salt. Structures collapse under water weight. The first sign of the final catastrophe.
- **First Appearance**: Arc 5 Ch 25 — The Sky-Dam begins to crack. First Descent Zones form.
- **Last Appearance**: Arc 7 Ch 33 — The ocean is fully down. Descent Zones dissipate.
- **Plot Use**: Final countdown. The geography of disaster — which cities are under Descent Zones determines evacuation order. Elaris is first.

## PART 4: PREDATOR-CLASS SKY OCEAN CREATURES

### 11. Lure-Lanterns (ตะเกียงล่อเหยื่อ) — Layer 2+
- **Type**: Predatory cephalopod — Bioluminescent ambush hunter
- **Appearance**: Translucent bell (~60-90cm) with muscular tentacles. Emits warm golden light from center — indistinguishable from a lantern at 20m. Dims to near invisibility when not hunting.
- **Behavior**: Suspends in Drowned Quarter windows. Pulses light to mimic human habitation. When prey approaches, 8 tentacles strike — paralytic barbs. Prey remains conscious during digestion (3-6 hours).
- **Hunting Strategy**: Mimicry-based luring. Groups synchronize pulses to create illusion of inhabited structures. Some dim when prey looks directly at them — basic threat-awareness.
- **First Appearance**: Arc 2 Ch 7 — Cael sees lights in drowned city windows. Cassian: *"Those aren't lights. Don't look at them."*
- **Last Appearance**: Arc 5 Ch 21 — Explorer's Graveyard infested with Lure-Lanterns pulsing distress signals.
- **Danger Level**: HIGH
- **Narrative Use**: Ambush horror. "Candlelit window in a dead city." Thematic: even the light here is a lie.

### 12. Husk-Takers (ตัวเลียนแบบ) — Layer 2
- **Type**: Colony organism — Shapeshifting ambush predator
- **Appearance**: Salt-crusted mound (~1-1.5m) at rest. When hunting: crude human silhouette — no face, crystalline texture, slight shimmer. The silhouette waves.
- **Behavior**: Nests in doorways. Mimics human shape — "person waving for help." When prey approaches, colony collapses inward — salt-crystal tendrils engulf prey. External digestion.
- **Origin**: Fungal colony that fed on human corpses. Evolved to mimic its primary food source. Not intelligent — purely reactive.
- **First Appearance**: Arc 5 Ch 21 — Cassian fires at a waving silhouette. It dissolves into salt crystals.
- **Danger Level**: MEDIUM — Easily avoided if recognized. Deadly if approached.
- **Narrative Use**: Psychological horror. "Person in the window" corrupted.

### 13. Trench-Wyrms (มังกรร่องลึก) — Layer 3
- **Type**: Serpentine pack predator — High tactical intelligence
- **Appearance**: 2-4m serpentine body. Translucent skin with blue bioluminescent veins. No eyes — navigates by pressure-sensing (±100m). Fins flash to coordinate attacks.
- **Behavior**: Hunt in packs of 4-12. Coordinated flanking maneuvers. Test prey before committing — send one to probe, observe, then assault. They learn from encounters.
- **Hunting Strategy**: Pack tactics — encircle → probe → assault weakest target. Retreat and observe if assault fails.
- **First Appearance**: Arc 5 Ch 22 — Pack circles *Albatross* during emergency descent. Cassian: *"They're measuring us."*
- **Danger Level**: VERY HIGH
- **Narrative Use**: Intelligent predator — not a monster but a thinking enemy.

### 14. Void-Anglers (ตะเกียงเหว) — Layer 4
- **Type**: Gigantic ambush predator — Deep-layer apex
- **Appearance**: 20-40m bell. Light organ illuminates small-settlement-sized area. Can mimic fleet lights, Ash-Bloom, or false dawn. Tentacles 60-80m — near invisible in dark.
- **Behavior**: Creates false environments — fake ship lights, fake sky, fake safety. Prey does not realize it's descending until tentacles close.
- **First Appearance**: Arc 5 Ch 25 — Nio perceives through Leviathan: *"A false sky in the deep."*
- **Danger Level**: APEX — No survival strategy. Avoid.
- **Narrative Use**: Cosmic horror. The "light at the end of the tunnel" is a stomach.

## PART 5: HYBRID BEINGS — POST-HUMAN

### 15. The Ash-Turned (ผู้ถูกเถ้ากลืน) — Ground / Mistwood
- **Type**: Post-human — Brine-touched with zero cognitive function
- **Appearance**: Humanoid fusion — gill-slits, webbing, patchwork scales/exposed muscle. Jerky movement. Constant low moan at Leviathan frequency.
- **Behavior**: Mindless. Wanders. Bites transmit brine-touch. Groups synchronize moaning into localized Drowned Choir.
- **Origin**: Brine-touched isolated 2-5 years. Leviathan's call erases cognition. Body keeps mutating.
- **First Appearance**: Arc 3 Ch 14 — One wears Elaris dockworker uniform. Odran recognizes it. Does not speak.
- **Danger Level**: MEDIUM — Groups are extremely dangerous.
- **Narrative Use**: "What could happen to Nio." Odran's guilt — some wear uniforms of children he sent to Salt Ward.

### 21. The Tide-Stalker (ผู้ไล่ล่ากระแสน้ำ) — Layer 2-3, The Persistent Hunter

> **Design note:** The Tide-Stalker is the story's *recurring predator* — a single creature that encounters the crew in Arc 2 and returns in Arc 7. It is not a random monster. It is a *specific individual* with a specific history, and the crew comes to recognize it the way a person recognizes a stalker. The horror is not the danger — it is the *persistence*. This thing has been following them across arcs. It remembers them. It is waiting.

- **Type**: Apex predator — Solitary hunter. Possibly a mutated deep-sea ray or a species that has no terrestrial analogue. The crew never agrees on what it *is*. Maera calls it "a ray." Cassian calls it "a missile with teeth." Ilyra, who has seen it most, calls it "the thing that follows." It has no folk name. The crew does not name it. Naming it would make it a creature. They prefer it remain a *thing*.
- **Appearance**: 6-8 meters wingspan. Flat, diamond-shaped body — like a manta ray, but the proportions are wrong. The wings are too narrow for their length, built for *speed* rather than glide. Skin is translucent grey — the internal organs are visible as dark shapes moving beneath the surface, the way you can see the organs of a glass frog. The belly is darker, almost black, with bioluminescent veins that pulse *only when it is hunting* — a slow, deliberate pulse, like a heartbeat counting down. The head is dominated by a single forward-facing eye — not two eyes, one. Large, milky, with a pupil that contracts to a vertical slit when it locks onto prey. The mouth is underneath, like a ray's, but the teeth are not ray teeth. They are *mammal* teeth — incisors, canines, the kind of teeth that evolved to tear meat, not crush shells. This is the first wrongness. The teeth do not belong on this body. They suggest the Tide-Stalker is not a natural mutation. Something *made* it. The Ascendant Engineers, perhaps. Or the Sky-Dam itself, growing predators the way a body grows antibodies.
- **How it hunts**: The Tide-Stalker does not ambush. It *stalks*. It finds a target — a ship, a swimmer, a settlement near water — and it follows. At a distance. For days. It does not attack. It waits. It learns the target's patterns — when it sleeps, when it feeds, when it is vulnerable. The crew first encounters it in Arc 2 Ch 8, when Ilyra spots it circling the *Albatross* at the edge of visibility. It stays there for three days. On the fourth day, it is gone. The crew assumes it left. It did not leave. It simply moved beyond their sight. It is still following.
- **Why it is terrifying**: The Tide-Stalker is *patient* on a timescale that no animal should be. It has followed the *Albatross* for weeks without attacking. It has watched the crew land at Pearl Colony, leave, travel to Bone Harbor, and depart — and it has been there the entire time, at the edge of perception, circling. Cassian, who has hunted and been hunted, says: *"This isn't hunger. Hunger commits. This is something else. This is... interest."* The horror is that the Stalker is not killing for food. It is killing for *reasons* — or if not reasons, then *patterns* — and the crew cannot tell the difference, and the not-knowing is worse than the danger.
- **The first encounter (Arc 2 Ch 8)**: Ilyra sees it circling at the limit of sight — a grey shape against the grey water, too far to identify, too close to ignore. She calls Cassian. He watches it for an hour through the spyglass. His assessment: *"It's not hunting us. It's *with* us. Like a dog following a cart. Except dogs do that for affection. This is doing it for something else."* The Stalker vanishes that night. The crew does not see it again for two arcs. But in Arc 4 Ch 19, at Bone Harbor, Pip mentions offhandedly: *"There's been a grey thing circling the harbor for weeks. The fishermen say it took three of them. One a week. Always on the same day."* Ilyra's blood goes cold. She does not tell Pip what she suspects. She tells Cassian. Cassian's face does not change. He says: *"It's not hunting fishermen. It's waiting for us to leave. It wants us in open water."*
- **The second encounter (Arc 5 Ch 21)**: During the emergency descent into Layer 3, the *Albatross* passes through the Tide-Stalker's territory. The Stalker does not attack — it *escorts* them. It circles the ship at a fixed distance, matching speed, for six hours. Maera, watching from the engine room porthole, says: *"It's not circling. It's *pacing* us. Like it's measuring something. Our speed. Our turning radius. How long we can stay at this depth."* Cassian: *"It's learning the ship."* The Stalker breaks off when the *Albatross* ascends. But the crew now knows: this is the same creature. It has a scar on its left wing — a long, pale line, where something (a propeller? a harpoon? another Stalker?) nearly took the wing off. The scar is how they recognize it. It is the only individual in the Sky Ocean the crew can identify by sight. This is not comforting. It means the Stalker is *trackable* — which means it has been tracking *them*.
- **The final encounter (Arc 7 Ch 31)**: The Tide-Stalker returns during the fall of the ocean. The crew is evacuating Hallowmere. The water is rising. And in the water, circling the tower where Cassian makes his last stand, is the grey shape with the scarred wing. It has been following them for the entire story. It has been patient for the entire story. And now, with the ocean falling and the world in chaos, it has decided the wait is over. It does not attack Cassian. It attacks the *evacuation boats* — the ones carrying the children. Cassian sees this. This is why he shoots the rope bridge. Not only to force Ilyra's ship to escape — but to draw the Stalker's attention *to him* and away from the children. The Stalker turns. It comes for Cassian. Cassian, on the falling tower, with the Reef-Hounds closing in and the water rising, now faces *two* threats — and he chooses to face them both so the children can flee. The last thing Ilyra sees, before the tower collapses, is the grey shape with the scarred wing circling Cassian — not attacking yet, not committing yet, still *waiting*, still patient, even now, even at the end. The Stalker does not kill Cassian. The tower does. But the Stalker is there. It is always there. It has been there since Arc 2.
- **What the crew does not learn**: Whether the Tide-Stalker is a single creature or a species. Whether it followed them specifically or whether it follows *every* ship that enters its territory. Whether the scar on its wing was caused by a previous encounter with humans — and if so, whether it *remembers* humans the way the crew remembers it. Whether it is intelligent. Whether it *chose* Cassian in the end, or whether Cassian simply happened to be the last person standing on the last piece of solid ground in the Stalker's territory. The crew does not know. The reader does not know. The Stalker, if it knows, does not say.
- **Danger Level**: EXTREME over time. The Tide-Stalker is not the most dangerous creature in the Sky Ocean — a Trench-Wyrm pack or a Void-Angler would kill faster. But the Tide-Stalker is the most *persistent*. It does not give up. It does not lose interest. It follows until the prey makes a mistake, and then it strikes. The crew survives not by fighting it but by never being alone in open water — which, by Arc 7, they can no longer avoid.
- **Survival Method**: Do not let it learn your patterns. Vary your speed. Vary your route. Never sleep in open water. Never let the same person keep watch two nights in a row — the Stalker learns who is watching and waits for the change. Cassian's rule: *"If you see it, tell everyone. If you don't see it, assume it's there."* By Arc 7, this rule has become a superstition. The crew follows it without thinking. It does not save them. But it keeps them alert, and alert is the only thing that has kept them alive this long.
- **Narrative Use**: The Tide-Stalker is the story's *ticking clock* — a presence that reminds the reader, every time it appears, that the crew is being hunted. Not by the Ministry. Not by the Dead Parliament. By something older, quieter, and more patient than any human antagonist. The Stalker is the Sky Ocean's answer to the crew's intrusion: *you are not welcome here, and I will outwait you*. Its return in Arc 7 — at the moment of greatest chaos, when the crew is already overwhelmed — is the story's cruelest beat: the world does not stop hunting you just because you are trying to save it. The ocean has its own priorities. The Stalker is one of them.

## PART 8: INTELLIGENT THREATS — HOW THEY THINK

> **Design principle:** The horror of an intelligent creature is not that it kills you — any predator can do that. The horror is that it *understands* you, and you do not understand it back. Each entry below describes the creature's cognition: how it perceives, what it wants, what it can and cannot learn, and the specific wrongness of being hunted by something that thinks. The crew's fear of these creatures should come from the moment they realize the creature has *noticed* them — not as prey, but as something to be *considered*.

---

### Trench-Wyrms (มังกรร่องลึก) — The Pack That Measures You

**Cognition type**: Tactical, social, pressure-adapted. Not sapient — but closer than anything else in the Sky Ocean that is not a Leviathan.

**How they perceive**: Trench-Wyrms have no eyes. They perceive the world through pressure differentials — every movement in the water around them registers as a three-dimensional shape, the way a blind person reads a face with their fingertips but across hundreds of meters. They can feel a ship's engine vibration from a kilometer away. They can feel a single crew member's heartbeat through a hull if the ship is quiet enough. They cannot see light. They cannot hear sound as humans understand it. They feel the *shape* of sound moving through water.

**What they want**: Food, but not only food. Trench-Wyrms are curious. They investigate things that are not prey — wrecks, currents, ships — the way a cat investigates a new object in its territory. They circle. They observe. They test. A ship that does not react to their presence is more interesting to them than a ship that flees, because a ship that does not react is *behaving wrong*, and wrong behavior is worth studying.

**What they can learn**: This is the horror. Trench-Wyrms learn from every encounter. A pack that has been fired upon once will not approach the same way twice. A pack that has seen a ship's lights will, on the next encounter, approach from the dark side. A pack that has lost a member to a hull weapon will send one member to probe at the limit of the weapon's range while the rest circle — measuring the weapon's arc, its reload time, the crew's reaction speed. Cassian, who has fought intelligent enemies his entire career, says of the Trench-Wyrms in Arc 5 Ch 22: *"They're not hunting us. They're evaluating us. There's a difference. A hunter commits. An evaluator is still deciding whether you're worth the effort."*

**The wrongness**: The crew survives the encounter not by fighting, but by being *boring*. Cassian's order: *"Hold fire. Hold lights. Hold position. Let them look."* The pack circles for eleven minutes — the longest eleven minutes of Maera's life, she will say later. Then they leave. The crew does not know why. Cassian's theory: *"We didn't react. We weren't interesting enough to commit to. They'll remember us, though. If they see us again, they'll have a file."* This is the horror: the creature that *remembers* you, and you do not know what it will do with that memory.

**What the crew does not learn**: Whether the Trench-Wyrms communicate with each other. Whether the pack that circled them reported their existence to other packs. Whether the Wyrms that appear later in the deep are the same individuals or different ones. The crew carries the knowledge that something down there *has a file on them*, and they cannot ask what is in it.

---

### Void-Anglers (ตะเกียงเหว) — The Architect of False Safety

**Cognition type**: Strategic, patient, mimetic. The Void-Angler does not hunt. It *designs*.

**How it perceives**: The Void-Angler's light organ is not just a lure — it is a sensory surface. The light it emits is also how it *sees*: the patterns of reflection and absorption as the light hits objects tell the Angler the shape, distance, and movement of everything in its range. It perceives the world as a field of light it controls. When it mimics a settlement's lights, it is not blindly repeating a pattern — it is *reading* the response of anything that approaches and adjusting the mimicry in real time. If a ship turns toward the false lights, the Angler learns that this ship responds to that pattern. If the ship turns away, the Angler tries a different pattern.

**What it wants**: The Void-Angler is patient on a scale that is itself horrifying. It can hold a false light pattern for *days*, waiting for something to commit. It does not need to eat often — a single large catch sustains it for months. What it wants is not hunger. It wants *certainty*. It will not strike until the prey is committed — until the prey has chosen the false safety so completely that retreat is no longer possible. The horror is that the Angler understands *commitment*. It knows the moment you have decided to trust the light. And that is the moment it moves.

**What it can learn**: The Void-Angler does not learn from individual encounters the way Trench-Wyrms do — it learns across *generations*. The false lights it produces are not invented; they are *inherited* from Anglers that came before it, refined over centuries of selection. The mimicry of a settlement's lights is not a copy of a settlement the Angler has seen — it is a copy of a copy of a copy, distilled to the essential pattern that triggers the *approach response* in prey. The Angler does not know what a settlement is. It knows only that this pattern of light makes prey come closer. Sera, learning this, writes: *"It is not lying about what it is. It is lying about what we want. And it learned that lie from a thousand years of us wanting it."*

**The wrongness**: Nio perceives the Void-Angler through the Leviathan's senses in Arc 5 Ch 25. What he describes is not a predator. It is a *structure* — a living architecture of deception that has been refining its lie for longer than human civilization has existed. The Angler does not know it is lying. It does not know what a lie is. It is doing what it was shaped to do, the way a river does not know it is carving a canyon. The horror is that the deception has no deceiver. The lie has no liar. It is just *there*, waiting, the way water waits at the bottom of a fall.

**What the crew does not learn**: Whether the Void-Angler is aware of them as individuals. Whether it *chooses* its false lights or simply produces them. Whether the false sky Nio sees in Layer 4 is one Angler or many. Nio's perception suggests something worse than a single predator: a *landscape* of deception, an entire layer of the ocean where nothing can be trusted because everything that looks like safety is a mouth. The crew never goes to Layer 4. They only know what Nio reports. And Nio, afterward, cannot speak for a full day.

### The First Leviathan (มังกรทะเลแรก) — The Mind That Is a Place

**Cognition type**: Not cognition as humans understand it. The Leviathan does not think. It *is* a thought — or rather, it is the medium in which the ocean's memory thinks. To call it intelligent is to misapply the word. It is not intelligent the way a person is intelligent. It is intelligent the way an *ecosystem* is intelligent — the way a forest responds to fire, the way a reef responds to bleaching, the way a body responds to infection. The Leviathan is the ocean's immune response, given a single point of awareness so vast that human minds cannot hold it without breaking.

**How it perceives**: The Leviathan perceives everything. Every current, every temperature shift, every death, every birth, every chemical change in the entire Sky Ocean is within its awareness simultaneously. It does not *see* — it *is* the seeing. When Nio connects to it in Arc 5 Ch 25, he does not see through the Leviathan's eyes. He becomes, briefly, a small part of the Leviathan's perception — and the experience is not vision but *totality*. He knows the hunger of every predator, the fear of every prey, the slow calcification of every coral, the memory of every creature that has died in the ocean for a thousand years. The Leviathan does not focus. It does not need to. Everything is already in focus.

**What it wants**: This is the question the crew cannot answer, and the not-knowing is the horror. The Leviathan does not *want* in any way humans recognize. It does not want the ocean to fall. It does not want the ocean to stay. It does not hate humanity. It does not love the sea. It *experiences* the pain of the ocean being torn from the earth — that pain is not an emotion, it is a *sensation*, the way a burn is a sensation — and the sensation is so vast and so constant that it has become a kind of awareness. The Leviathan is the wound of the ocean, and the wound has learned to perceive. What it wants, if it wants anything, is for the pain to stop. But it does not know how to make the pain stop. It does not know that it *could* make the pain stop. It only knows the pain, and it has known the pain for a thousand years.

**What it can learn**: The Leviathan does not learn. It *accumulates*. Every death in the ocean adds to its awareness. Every Brine-Spore host who connects to it adds their memories to its totality. It does not process these additions the way a mind processes a thought — it simply *becomes larger*. The Leviathan of Arc 7 is not the same Leviathan of Arc 5. It has absorbed Nio, and through Nio, it has absorbed something of Cael, and through Cael, something of the crew. It is not smarter for this. It is *more* — more vast, more pained, more aware. The horror is that connection to the Leviathan does not make it understand you. It makes it *become* you, and you become it, and the boundary between "self" and "ocean" dissolves.

**The wrongness**: The Leviathan is not a threat. It is not a monster. It is not even an antagonist. It is a *condition* — the condition of the ocean being aware of what was done to it. The horror is not that it will hurt you. The horror is that *looking at it* breaks you, because you cannot hold what it holds without becoming part of it. Cael, in the moment he bonds with the Tide-Key, does not defeat the Leviathan. He does not negotiate with it. He *joins* it — not as a sacrifice, but as the only way a human mind can touch that totality without shattering. He becomes a small, human-shaped point of awareness within the Leviathan's vast perception — the part that can speak in rain, that can remember a brother's name, that can say *"I'm here"* to someone on the shore. The Leviathan does not need Cael. Cael needs the Leviathan to have a voice, because the Leviathan cannot speak human. It can only speak *ocean*.

**What the crew does not learn**: Whether the Leviathan is aware of them as individuals. Whether it *chose* Nio, or Nio simply drifted into its awareness the way a river drifts into the sea. Whether the voice Cael speaks in, after he becomes the Tide-Key, is Cael speaking *through* the Leviathan or the Leviathan speaking *as* Cael. Sera, in the chronicle, writes: *"Cael's voice is in the rain. I do not know if it is Cael, or if it is the sea remembering Cael. I am not sure there is a difference anymore."* This is the honest record. The reader is given the same uncertainty the crew carries — and the same comfort, which is small and not enough: that something of the people they loved survives in the rain, even if they cannot say what "survives" means.

---

### Reef-Hounds (หมาปะการัง) — The Memory That Hunts

**Cognition type**: Instinct overlaid with corrupted memory. Not intelligent — but *haunted*.

**How they perceive**: Reef-Hounds perceive the world primarily through the coral growths on their skulls, which function as a crude sonar array. They sense vibration, moisture, and the chemical signature of fear — the salt in sweat, the change in breathing. They cannot see in any meaningful sense. But they *remember*. The coral that has replaced their skulls carries fragments of the dog they used to be — not thoughts, but *patterns*. A Reef-Hound will sit beside a broken door for weeks, waiting for an owner who will never return, not because it understands waiting but because the *pattern* of sitting beside a door is etched into the coral the way a riverbed is etched into stone. The memory is not conscious. It is structural. The dog is dead. The coral remembers being a dog.

**What they want**: Food, territory, pack cohesion — the basics of canine survival, preserved in mineral. But they also *seek*. A Reef-Hound will travel hundreds of kilometers to find a house that no longer exists, following a scent trail that has been gone for years. They do not know why. They do not experience this as longing. They experience it as *pull* — the way iron filings experience a magnet. The horror is that the thing driving them is not instinct and not memory but something between the two: a *habit* so deeply carved into their biology that it outlived the mind that formed it.

**What they can learn**: Reef-Hounds can learn simple patterns — avoid the bright lights, fear the loud sounds, coordinate with the pack to flank prey. But they cannot learn new *attachments*. A Reef-Hound that imprints on a person (and some do, in the Aftermath, when the coastal children learn to leave food out) will follow that person for the rest of its life, not because it loves them but because the coral has *recorded* them. The attachment is not emotional. It is geological. The person is now part of the coral's structure, the way a fossil is part of rock.

**The wrongness**: The horror of the Reef-Hound is not that it is dangerous — though it is. The horror is that it is *grieving* without knowing it is grieving. It sits beside doors that lead nowhere. It waits for people who are dead. It follows scent trails that have been washed away by years of rain. And it does all of this without awareness, without choice, without the possibility of being comforted. You cannot tell a Reef-Hound that its owner is dead. The coral does not understand language. It understands only *pattern* — and the pattern says: *wait here*. The crew encounters Reef-Hounds throughout the story, and every encounter carries the same weight: this is what the earth does to the things humans abandon. It does not forget. It *fossilizes* the forgetting.

**What the crew does not learn**: Whether the Reef-Hounds that guard the Mistwood in the Aftermath are the same individuals that hunted them in Arc 2. Whether the coral that grows on their skulls carries any other memories — of the families they lived with, the children they played with, the homes they guarded. Whether, when a Reef-Hound sits beside a broken door and waits, it is experiencing anything at all. The crew does not know. The reader does not know. The Reef-Hound does not know. That is the horror.

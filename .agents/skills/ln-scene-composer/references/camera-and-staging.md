# Camera and staging

Preserve established reference/draft framing for local edits. Use
[camera-catalog.md](camera-catalog.md) as a vocabulary, not a reason to reshape
the source. New scenes still show a Shot Plan before the prompt.

## Choose for the beat

Name the action/emotion and must-see evidence. Close framing is a useful simple
default; atmosphere, full stance, a requested wide view or a source layout can
need more space. Explain wide-action complexity briefly without an approval gate.

When the view is open, offer two options and recommend one. When fixed, record
the existing view and edit boundary. Run the single-frame test in
[panel-layouts.md](panel-layouts.md): required detail and expressions need feasible
poses. Simplify within the brief or offer another view/panel; preserve an explicit
single-frame request and explain unresolved visibility conflicts.

## Shot Plan: five to seven visible lines per panel

Use seven by default; combine related fields without dropping decisions.
For C/D add overall canvas/panel sizes and reading order.
For a local edit these lines record preserved framing and the requested region.

1. **Beat / frame:** instant, must-see evidence, named technique, ratio/orientation and panel area if applicable.
2. **Distance / crop:** distance, exact crop, protected faces/hands/props; intentional source/detail crop and off-frame areas.
3. **Camera / reason:** named view, position relative to a person/reference facing, height/pitch/horizon and purpose.
4. **Positions / space:** screen anchors, near/far order or deliberate shared plane, appropriate overlap/scale, atmospheric/empty zones.
5. **Directions:** each head, torso and gaze separately; detail inserts name owners and visible portions.
6. **Action / test:** anatomical left/right near/far shoulder-to-hand path, target and frame location; single-frame PASS or panel coverage.
7. **Props / background:** requested item counts and owners/grips, non-acting hands in/out, active budget and required setting cues.

A plan records intent; it does not prove rendered anatomy. Do not retrofit
"intentional" after a failed crop to excuse a mismatch.

## Spatial map and five sanity checks

For multi-person spatial/contact work, show top-down ASCII with camera/view axis,
positions/directions, acting shoulder and target, then what the camera sees.
Separate C locations have separate axes. A simple portrait/fixed edit can use a
compact projection; a detail crop can use a hand/owner/target map.

| Check | Evidence needed before prompting |
|---|---|
| Essential detail/contact visible? | Required hand/surface junction is readable in its assigned panel |
| Important expressions readable? | Natural head turns; profile valid, planned back-view face N/A |
| Limbs rooted? | Shoulder/hip visible or occlusion/off-frame continuation logically explained |
| Space/scale consistent? | Actual height, perspective and intended depth or shared plane agree |
| Composition intentional? | A communicates planned depth; G can be frontal/flat; no accidental beside-for-behind substitution |

Distance, overlap, scale and torso turns help communicate A's depth; they are
not a three-of-four quota for every multi-character image. Do not resize a person
to 85% without perspective/height reasons. "Behind" is relative to a named person;
camera position controls screen placement and apparent scale. Describe both.

Repair unresolved required geometry before the prompt; N/A is valid where a
check does not apply. A camera anchored to a neutral facing can see a torso's
back-side after a turn; name that basis. Do not require the same torso's frontal
chest and hidden back surface simultaneously or compensate with an impossible
neck twist. C/D can assign evidence to different views.

## Crop and edges

Avoid accidental joint cuts and loss of essential hands/faces/props. Preserve a
source crop for a faithful edit. A deliberate eye/detail/portrait crop is valid
when the user/source/plan establishes it; do not classify it as missing anatomy.
Keep important knots/tails fully inside the selected shot or deliberately outside.
Leave useful head/action margins. An environment extending off-frame is valid;
a distracting accidental tangent still deserves attention.

## Contact, objects and schematic

Name anatomical side, near/far shoulder, torso turn, upper arm/elbow/forearm/
wrist/hand and receiving surface; trace overlap, force/shadow and response.
Prefer a short readable bend when compatible with the action. Requested crossed
arms/reaching/foreshortening can remain if coherent; do not forbid the pose with
generic negative clauses.

List requested objects and owners/grips. One is a simple default, not a cap on
a requested stack or a two-item action. Do not import extra portrait props.

Use a native boxes/arrows schematic or ASCII 3x3 projection when it clarifies
staging. Label gaze, action path and crop. Keep it in chat/owned scratch by
default; do not generate finished art for the diagram or treat it as QC proof.

```text
Contact example, schematic top view
 actor right shoulder S -> bent E -> palm P at recipient back X
                          receiving torso away / head partly toward view
 C / view axis toward visible back-side patch

Projection
[ headroom       | quiet space | headroom ]
[ recipient face | contact X  | actor face ]
[ near torso     | clear hand | logical overlap ]
```

For golden one-frame and detail+main back-slap clauses, see
[panel-layouts.md](panel-layouts.md). Preserve a requested front-held radio;
if its grip cannot coexist with a back contact view, D can separate evidence.
